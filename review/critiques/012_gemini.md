# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This is exactly the kind of crunchy, high-stress management game I want to sink my teeth into. Taking the FTL power-routing formula and turning it vertical for a descent into a hollow Earth is a fantastic pitch. The high-resolution stills show a beautifully grungy, industrial art style that nails the claustrophobic vibe. The UI is intimidatingly dense, which I secretly love, though the actual act of drilling feels a bit weightless in motion right now.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] Crew are too static. In the video, they just stand idle at their stations. I want to see them frantically typing, turning valves, or bracing themselves when the ship hits dense rock. 
2. [hurts] Drilling lacks physical impact. In the video, the rock chunks that break off look weightless and disappear too cleanly. I want heavy, grinding physics, persistent dust, and screen shake to sell the violence of the drill.
3. [nitpick] Gas hazard visual is weak. At 02:47 in the video, hitting the gas pocket just spawns a flat, expanding green blob. It needs to look like a volatile, dangerous chemical fire.
4. [nitpick] UI density. The left panel in the first image is very text-heavy. While I like the FTL layout, replacing some of the tiny sub-system text (speed, heat, noise) with clear iconography would speed up mid-panic reading.

## Working, don't touch
- The gritty, industrial art style and lighting in the high-res stills. The ship looks like a rusty, dangerous deathtrap, which is perfect.
- The room color-coding. The stark lighting differences between the reactor, coolant, and bracing rooms make the ship incredibly easy to read at a glance.
- The FTL-style power and crew management systems. It's a proven, highly engaging mechanic that translates perfectly to this setting.
