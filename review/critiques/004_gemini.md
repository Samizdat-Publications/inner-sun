# Player Review

## Gut reaction
I am a sucker for FTL, and "FTL but down" is a fantastic pitch that immediately makes sense to me. The core loop of routing power, moving crew, and dealing with cascading failures is instantly recognizable and engaging. However, right now it looks and feels exactly like an early prototype. The interior art is so noisy I can't find my crew, the "drilling" feels like an elevator riding past scrolling wallpaper, and the water physics are hilariously bad. I'm very interested in the systems, but it needs a massive visual overhaul to feel like a real, physical place.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The water hazard is a glaring placeholder. It's a bright, translucent blue box with a rigid sine-wave top that just sits over the room (Frame 2, video 0:17). It doesn't interact with the walls, machines, or crew. I want water that fills the space, reacts to movement, and looks like dirty coolant or groundwater, not a UI overlay.
2. [hurts] The "Earth" we are drilling through is a flat, endlessly tiling rock texture (Frame 1). It feels like an elevator moving past wallpaper, not a massive drill grinding through the planet's crust. I want to see different strata, sparks flying, debris, and a sense of immense friction and destruction outside the hull.
3. [hurts] The combat staging makes no physical sense. In the video (2:29), the rival rig just floats next to us, and we shoot projectiles horizontally through what should be solid rock. It feels completely disconnected from the drilling theme. I want combat to feel like two drills violently intersecting or fighting in a carved-out cavern, not floating in a void.
4. [hurts] Readability inside the ship is poor. The backgrounds are so heavily detailed and uniformly brown/rusty that the darkly-dressed crew members completely disappear into them (Frame 1, Reactor room). I have to rely entirely on the UI to know where they are. I want better rim lighting or contrast so the crew pops out from the machinery.
5. [nitpick] The UI takes up way too much space. The actual ship is crammed into the center third of the screen, surrounded by massive, chunky panels. I want the ship and the environment to be the star, with the UI framing it, not dominating it.

## Working, don't touch
- The core loop of power routing, crew management, and targeting systems is instantly familiar and fun.
- The node-based map and text events fit the pulp conspiracy vibe perfectly.
- The warm, glowing light coming from the drill bits at the bottom of the ship looks great.
