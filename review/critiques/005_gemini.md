# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This is basically FTL aimed straight down into the earth's crust, and as a systems-heavy roguelite fan, I am completely on board with that pitch. The UI and crew management instantly make sense to me, and the gritty, detailed art of the ship's interior looks fantastic. However, the illusion of drilling deep underground is currently shattered by a scrolling rock background that looks like a cheap 90s screensaver. If they can nail the atmosphere and terrain, this could easily eat 100 hours of my life.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The rock texture (all still frames). The repeating, horizontal wavy bands look like a cheap scrolling wallpaper and completely clash with the beautiful ship art. I want chunky, jagged, or varied terrain that actually looks like solid earth being crushed.
2. [hurts] The ship silhouette is a perfect rectangle (all frames). A 2x3 grid of perfect squares feels like a spreadsheet, not a vehicle built to withstand immense subterranean pressure. I want angled outer hulls, varied room sizes, or some structural bulkheads.
3. [hurts] Lighting is too safe and bright (still frames). The area around the ship is entirely visible and evenly lit. I want oppressive, claustrophobic darkness outside, relying on the drill's heat and the ship's external spotlights to see the immediate surroundings.
4. [nitpick] The purple gas/hazard (video, bottom right room) looks a bit like a generic particle emitter. It needs to feel more integrated into the high-res, gritty aesthetic of the rooms.

## Working, don't touch
- The UI layout and terminal aesthetic. Borrowing FTL's homework was the right call; it's perfectly readable and fits the theme.
- The highly detailed, gritty art style of the ship interiors and the glowing, sparking drill bit.
- The node-based map system. It's a proven formula that works excellently here.
