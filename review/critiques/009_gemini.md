# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This pitch is basically made for me - an FTL-style management game mixed with Dome Keeper/Motherload drilling sounds incredible. But right now, the presentation is fighting the gameplay. The UI dominates the screen, the hazards look like MS Paint placeholders, and the manual mining section feels completely out of place. I want to feel the claustrophobia of a metal can diving into the earth, but right now it feels like I'm playing a spreadsheet with a tiny flash game running in the middle.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The hazard effects are immersion-breaking placeholders. At 00:40, the water flooding the Coolant room is just a flat, rising blue block. Fire (00:32) is a static-y loop that doesn't cast light. I need to see splashing, bubbling, and dynamic lighting to feel the danger.
2. [hurts] The manual mining looks terrible. From 01:20 to 01:50, a crew member goes outside and just wiggles a pickaxe sprite against a rock block. It looks like a cheap web game and completely breaks the gritty, high-stakes management vibe of the ship interior.
3. [hurts] The UI-to-gameplay ratio is way off. Look at Frame 1: the UI takes up roughly 60-70% of the screen real estate. The actual ship is a tiny box in the center. The ship needs to be the star of the show.
4. [hurts] Crew readability is poor. The sprites are so small and lack distinct silhouettes that I can't tell Vasquez from Okafor without looking at the crew panel on the right. 
5. [nitpick] Drilling lacks physical impact. From 00:00 to 00:07, the drill spins and sparks, but the rock background just smoothly scrolls upward. It doesn't feel like we are tearing through the earth's crust; it feels like an elevator ride.

## Working, don't touch
- The overall concept and node-based map (00:13) are a perfect fit for this genre.
- The room art inside the ship (Reactor, Drill Room) is great. They have distinct color coding (blue glow, orange machinery) that makes the ship itself visually interesting.
- The writing and tone in the text events ("Reel Seven" at 00:08, the shop at Frame 4) perfectly nail that bureaucratic, Soviet-pulp conspiracy vibe.

## What I want next
Make the hazards look terrifying. If a pipe bursts and water fills the room, I want it to look like a desperate, drowning situation, not a rising blue progress bar. Shrink the UI and zoom in on the ship so I can actually see my crew fighting for their lives.
