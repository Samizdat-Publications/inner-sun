The Making of Inner Sun

A drill tower bores down through a hollow Earth toward the place where the UAPs are built. This is the build history from the first grey box, logged like a borehole: one core sample per moment, newest at the bottom. It updates as work lands.

Seven reviews

Each review comes from a critic that knows nothing about the code and judges a captured run like a player would. The amber points are Gemini watching video: 5.6 before the digging rework, 6.8 on build 3, 4.5 on the first cut of build 4 (the rock read as "a scrolling screen saver of wavy sand"), 6.1 once the rock was redrawn.

Kickoff15 Oct

A drill ship into a hollow Earth

Stewart pitches an FTL-style roguelite: a black-budget drill ship, crew and power to manage, a node map, events, fights, and something following your noise. The tall tower is chosen over a horizontal mole, so depth is the whole picture.

"My favorite games are digging games where you collect ore and gems and upgrade your gear."

M015 Oct

The loop proven

Before any art: a fire drill in placeholder rooms, a bot playing it, frames captured off-screen, and the same result hash headless and on camera. Proof that Claude can build, play and look at the game on its own.

M1 begins15 Oct

The critic sees 3.8 out of 10

A blind reviewer is installed. Its first verdict: a debug view, not a game yet. The exposure is lifted so it can see anything at all.

Art15 Oct

The bake-off picks a style

Painted pulp against Blender pre-renders, the same reactor room lit the same way in Godot. The painted rooms win; HDR glow finally blooms.

Screens15 Oct

A HUD, a dossier, a map

The power panel, the seismic scanner, crew cards, the typewritten dossier with redaction bars and TOPSIDE's radio strip, and the node map with the pursuit front creeping down.

Alive15 Oct

The ship comes alive, then fights

Painted rooms, props and crew replace the grey boxes. Then two drill towers trade fire through the rock.

M1a16 Oct

A whole run, handed to Stewart

Map, drilling, events, a store, fights, death and the codex, all playable. The critic climbs to 6.1. A last audit catches stutter while drilling; repainting only the cut rock brings the worst frames from 31 to 78 fps.

Playtest16 Oct

Stewart plays: "I don't see anything I can do"

Two and a half minutes, steering only. Claude then plays it with real keyboard and mouse and finds why: the map clock kills you while you choose, power is locked during events, the first fight is unwinnable unprepared, and nothing explains the controls. Stewart picks a new direction: digging is the game, with fights now and then, the way space games mix scanning planets for resources with the occasional hostile.

"Each level is like 30 seconds and you get like one piece of coal, each run should be way longer."

M1b16 Oct

See the dig

Controls fixed and checked by hand: help on the first run, the map holds time, power works in dossiers, the fight pauses and says what to do. The camera pulls back so you see the rock you steer for; the rock gets strata, the water gets murky, the crew get bright harnesses. Underneath, a new economy: coal, iron, copper, silver and gold, quartz, amethyst and emerald, richer with depth, gas pockets that start fires, surveys on every map node, and ore that buys drill heads, plating and pumps.

Build 316 Oct

Playtest build 3: digging is the game

Ore shows in the main view and pops off the crown as you cut it, a tally counts the dig, the scanner colours every ore, and each map node carries a survey of the rock on the way there. The Crust grows to 14 rows: runs of about 20 minutes, three trading posts, two fights, and pressure that builds with depth. Gemini scores it 6.8, up from 5.6. Its one big complaint is still the rock texture, which is next.

"The core FTL-underground loop is incredibly strong." (Gemini, critic 005)

M1c16 Oct

"Going straight down" is not a game

Stewart reaches row 8 and calls build 3 "amazing so far", but the dig is "just picking a spot based on the radar and going straight down", and he cannot see what the helm or the bracing do. The answer: every powered system gets a job while drilling. The helm cuts sideways along a vein, bracing soaks rockfalls in fault rock, coolant douses fires, the charge launcher blasts rock at range, and the reactor never powers it all at once.

"I want more things to manage so it feels less on rails."

Build 416 Oct

Playtest build 4: things in the rock

Buried structures break up the descent: a timbered mine gallery with crates by the rails, a riveted Soviet rig module, a crystal hollow whose pool rises and falls with every other hollow, sometimes with a trader by a lamp. Every run now pays research toward permanent equipment for the next dive. The rock that Gemini twice called wallpaper is redrawn as fractured stone, and the world outside the hull scores 6 instead of 3.

"The sparks and breaking rocks are great." (Gemini, critic 007)

Build 517 Oct

Playtest build 5: a miner with a pick

From Stewart's build 3 notes: "explore a park and disembark to allow a single miner to mine out the veins". Press G, click a vein within 12 m, and the drill parks while one crew member walks to the drill room, climbs out with a headlamp and digs a tunnel by hand to mine the vein out. The haul pays only if they climb back in. Meanwhile they man nothing, gas, water and fault rock can hurt them, and the pursuit front keeps coming. The same night: a toast that lists your requisitions at the first dive, a crystal hollow lit blue instead of a black hole, and the rock's 1.28 m tile repeat broken up. Gemini, watching four minutes of a bot run, gives 5.6 and asks for fire that lights its room and a world with depth behind the rock.
